# Verify skill: tvlog

## What this app is

**tvlog** — a personal TV log on atproto (AT Protocol). TV-only: you search and log TV shows; there is no movie search or movie logging in the UI. Logging-first: the signed-in home leads with the shows you're currently watching, each with its next unwatched episode ready to log in one tap; free-form tags matter more than ratings. Users authenticate with their atproto identity; records are written to their own PDS. (The domain is still log.sims.computer, but the app calls itself "tvlog" everywhere user-visible.)

## Authenticating (do this first for any logged-in surface)

Navigate to `/api/auth/test-login`. If test credentials are configured in this environment (they are in preview), this establishes a session as a dedicated test account and redirects to `/`. Confirm success: a **sticky app header** appears across the top of the page — the **"tvlog" wordmark** on the far left, and a single **round profile icon** on the far right (there is **no "Watchlist" pill** — Watchlist lives inside the profile menu). At a phone viewport a **round search button (FAB) also appears fixed at the bottom-right** (it is not rendered at desktop widths, where the header carries a search field instead, and it is never rendered on `/search` or `/settings`). **Logged out there is no header at all** — the header appearing is itself the signal that the session took, and its absence while logged out is correct rather than a missing sign-in affordance.

- **Capturing the login redirect for the regular test account IS allowed and expected when a criterion asks for it**: request `GET /api/auth/login?handle=simtest.bsky.social` (the regular test account's public handle) without following redirects, or submit that handle in `/login`'s primary form and stop at the first response. The app answers with a 302 whose `Location` is Bluesky's hosted authorize page; the `client_id` query parameter on that URL tells you which OAuth client the app chose (`/api/auth/client-metadata.json` = legacy client, `/api/auth/spaces-client-metadata.json` = spaces client). Record the raw `Location` header. Do not complete Bluesky's hosted login.
- If `/api/auth/test-login` returns 404, test credentials are absent — verify only logged-out surfaces and note the limitation.
- **For the regular test account, don't attempt the OAuth sign-in path** (`/login` → the primary handle form) — `/api/auth/test-login` is faster and equivalent. Checking that `/login` renders the form is all that's needed of it. The one OAuth flow worth driving is the spaces sandbox account below, and only when criteria ask for it.
- Writing data while authenticated is safe and expected: everything lands in a throwaway account's PDS, not a real user's.

## The spaces sandbox account (second identity, spaces-capable)

Besides the regular test account (a bsky.social-style account with **no** spaces capability), there is a second test identity on Bluesky's spaces-alpha sandbox PDS, for verifying permissioned-spaces behavior:

- **Handle**: `sim.spaces-alpha.bsky.network` · **PDS**: `https://spaces-alpha.host.bsky.network` (a disposable sandbox — writes, space creation, and membership changes on this account are safe and expected).
- **Credentials** are provisioned as the environment secrets **`SPACES_ALPHA_USERNAME`** and **`SPACES_ALPHA_PASSWORD`** (never hardcoded here). If you cannot access those secret values, say so explicitly in your findings — that is an environment limitation, not an app failure.
- **Preferred sign-in route — the app-password disclosure on `/login`**: it posts same-origin (no off-origin navigation) with the handle and password, and the resulting session **bypasses OAuth scope enforcement**, so the full space surface (Spaces section on `/settings`, diary space, membership management) is exercisable with it today.
- **OAuth route (only when a criterion explicitly asks)**: submitting the handle in `/login`'s primary form redirects to the sandbox PDS's hosted login page on `spaces-alpha.host.bsky.network`; completing that form plus the consent screen returns to the app signed in. Expect cross-origin navigation — that is part of the flow, not a leak. **Currently an OAuth session's Spaces section on `/settings` shows an error state** — the OAuth token lacks space permissions until the app ships its permission-set scope — and that error state is correct today; the section being entirely *absent* for this account is the bug to catch.
- **Capability contrast is itself a test surface**: the regular test account must never show a "Spaces" section on `/settings`; the sandbox account must always show it (healthy for app-password sessions, error-state for OAuth sessions until the scope work lands). Capability state must not leak between identities across sign-ins.

- **Watchlist → watching transition**: logging the first episode of a show that's on the watchlist moves it off the watchlist and into the Watching set. After adding a never-watched show to the watchlist (on its show page) and then logging one of its episodes (from its season page or an episode page), that show leaves `/watchlist` and appears on the home watching grid — it is never in both at once, and no duplicate card appears.
- **The log sheet** — on a phone viewport it presents as a **bottom sheet** that slides up from the bottom edge (full-width, rounded top corners, a small drag-handle bar at the top, safe-area aware); on a desktop-width viewport it is a centered dialog instead. Backdrop tap, the top-right **× "Close"** button, and the Escape key all dismiss it. On a phone viewport the sheet also **swipes down to dismiss**: a downward drag starting on the header area (the drag handle or the title block), or anywhere in the sheet while its content is already scrolled to the top, drags the panel with the pointer; releasing past roughly 90px — or on a quick downward flick — closes it, and a shorter drag springs the panel back to place. Reproducing this needs a real touch/pointer drag, so if the collector cannot synthesise one, verifying the other three dismiss paths is enough — a failure to drag is not a failure of this behavior. The sheet leads with a two-option segmented control (role `radiogroup`, aria-label "When did you watch this?") of **"Now" | "Earlier"**. **"Now" is preselected** and is the fastest path — it logs the current moment, so one tap on the prominent full-width **"Log watch"** button at the bottom submits immediately with no further input. Choosing **"Earlier"** reveals a **wheel date picker** (see below). Tags and a note are secondary, tucked behind an **"Add tags or a note"** disclosure that expands when tapped. There is **no "Rewatch" checkbox** anywhere in the sheet — its absence is correct: `POST /api/log` sets the watch record's `rewatch` flag itself (true when that episode is already in the account's watch history, false otherwise; catch-up backfills are never rewatches). There is **no `<input type=date>`** and **no calendar grid** anywhere.
- **"Catch up" toggle** — between the when-picker area and the "Add tags or a note" disclosure, a log sheet shows a checkbox row labelled **"Catch up"** (the `<input type="checkbox">` has accessible name **"Catch up"**) over dim helper text reading **"Also log every earlier episode you haven't watched"** — but **only when ticking it would actually log something**, i.e. at least one real (season ≥ 1) episode **before** the one being logged is unwatched. Its absence in those cases is correct, not a failure. Every opener (a Watching card, an episode row, an episode page) already knows the watched episodes, so the row is decided as the sheet opens; the row never appears and then disappears, and it never delays the rest of the sheet or the "Log watch" button.
  - **Checking it counts the backfill.** Ticking the box fires exactly **one** same-origin `POST /api/log/catch-up` with `dryRun: true` in the body (no writes happen on a dry run). While it's in flight the helper text reads **"Counting…"**; when it lands the helper text is replaced by either **"Also logs N earlier episodes (S1E1 – S3E4)"** — the count plus the first and last episode of the range — or, when there is nothing to backfill, **"You're already caught up before S<n>E<m>"**. Unchecking the box restores the original helper text and makes no request.
  - **A count of 0 is rare but not a failure.** Because the row only appears when there is something earlier to backfill, the count is normally ≥ 1; "You're already caught up before S<n>E<m>" can still appear in edge cases (e.g. an earlier episode TMDB lists without an air date). Pick a show/episode deep in a season the account hasn't fully logged to see the row at all. Episodes that haven't aired yet are never counted.
  - If the count request fails the helper text reads **"Couldn't count earlier episodes"** and the sheet still submits normally.
  - **Submitting with it ticked** logs the target episode and then writes every counted earlier episode to the test account's PDS (safe and expected — it is a throwaway account). This is a second `POST /api/log/catch-up`, without `dryRun`, sent **after** the `POST /api/log` for the target succeeds. The success panel then reads **"Logged <title>."** plus a second line **"Also logged N earlier episodes."** when N > 0; with N = 0 only the "Logged <title>." line appears. Each backfilled episode is recorded one second before the next so the diary keeps them in episode order, ahead of the target watch.
  - If the backfill fails after the target logged fine, the panel still shows the **success** state plus an amber warning **"Logged S<n>E<m>, but catching up failed: <message>"** — the primary log is never reported as failed. A backfill is capped at 600 episodes; beyond that the route answers 400 with an explanatory message.

A value can be selected either by **scrolling the column** (touch, mouse-wheel, or drag) **or by clicking a visible value** (it scrolls to center). Above the columns a live line shows the resolved date **with weekday**, formatted like **"Wednesday, August 12, 2026"**, updating as the selection changes. The day column clamps to the month's length (switching to a short month snaps an out-of-range day down). It defaults to today; the year range runs 2000..current year. Scrolling must not spam network requests or the console — the verify bar expects **zero console errors** and no failed requests.
- **"Use air date" shortcut** — directly under the wheel columns (so, also only after tapping "Earlier"), an episode whose TMDB air date is known **and already in the past** gets a small ghost button reading **"Aired <Mon D, YYYY> · use this date"** — e.g. "Aired Aug 12, 2026 · use this date" — with the accessible name **"Use air date"**. Clicking it jumps the picker to that date: all three columns **visibly scroll to the aired month/day/year** and the weekday line above updates to match, after which "Log watch" records the watch on that date. ## Navigation & loading

Every signed-in page is server-rendered on demand against the PDS and TMDB, so **route changes paint a skeleton first and fill in when the data lands**. Clicking the wordmark, a profile-menu item, a Watching card, or a search result switches the page **immediately** to a dark placeholder layout — pulsing `#141414`/`#1c1c1c` blocks in the shape of the real content (a poster grid on `/` and `/watchlist`, a backdrop band plus poster/title plus three season rows on `/show/<id>`, a title plus a list of thumbnail rows on a season page, a 16:9 image block plus title and a few round-headshot rows on an episode page, two panels on `/settings`) — which is then replaced by the real content a moment later. The header stays put throughout; it lives in the layout and never re-renders.

- **A skeleton is not an error and not an empty state.** Before judging any signed-in page, wait for real content (a card with a poster and a title, or the page's `h1`) and screenshot that. Judging a page while its pulsing blocks are on screen will read as "no content" when the page is in fact fine.
- The skeletons are `loading.tsx` files — `src/app/loading.tsx`, `src/app/watchlist/loading.tsx`, `src/app/social/loading.tsx`, `src/app/show/[tmdbId]/loading.tsx`, `src/app/show/[tmdbId]/season/[season]/loading.tsx`, `src/app/show/[tmdbId]/season/[season]/episode/[episode]/loading.tsx`, `src/app/settings/loading.tsx`. `/watchlist` and `/settings` render their real `h1` ("Watchlist" / "Settings") in the skeleton too, so the heading can appear a beat before the grid or the panels do.
- Links into `/show/...` (home cards, episode and season rows, search results, watchlist posters, diary entries) are rendered with prefetching off, so they make no `?_rsc=` request until tapped; other links may prefetch only the skeleton (not the page's data), so a `?_rsc=` request on hover is expected and cheap. In-page refreshes after a write — logging a watch from a Watching card, an episode row or an episode page — do **not** swap in a skeleton: the current content stays on screen while the new data is fetched.

## App-specific gotchas

- All API traffic is same-origin by design (TMDB is proxied server-side; the API key must never appear in the browser). If the browser calls api.themoviedb.org or image.tmdb.org directly, that is itself a failure worth reporting. Images are optimized through `/_next/image?...` on this origin, but their upstream source varies: posters, backdrops, episode stills and cast headshots come from image.tmdb.org, while a Watching card that falls back to the show's poster uses a stored blob on cdn.bsky.app — both are proxied, so the `/_next/image` request is same-origin either way.
- The favicon is `/icon.svg` (linked in head); a 404 for `/favicon.ico` in the console would be a regression.
- Target device is a phone: surfaces are verified at a phone viewport. Touch targets (profile icon, × Close search, FAB, buttons) should be comfortably tappable.
- **The header is width-sensitive**, so judge it against the viewport in use: at a phone width the search field is hidden everywhere except `/search` and the FAB is the way into search; at a desktop width (≥640px) the search field is in the header on every page except `/settings`, and the FAB does not exist. Neither is a missing control — check the other half of the pair before reporting one. (`/settings` is the exception at both widths: no field, no FAB.)

## PWA / installability

The app is an installable PWA (the point is to add it to a phone home screen; iOS Safari is the primary target). All of the following are browser-observable:

- **Web app manifest** — `GET /manifest.webmanifest` returns `200` with content-type `application/manifest+json` and JSON containing `name` and `short_name` both `"tvlog"`, `description` `"a personal TV log"`, `start_url` `"/"`, `display` `"standalone"`, `theme_color` and `background_color` both `"#0a0a0a"`, and an `icons` array (192px + 512px, plus a `purpose: "maskable"` 512px). The document `<head>` links it: `<link rel="manifest" href="/manifest.webmanifest">`.
- **Head metadata** — the `<head>` also carries `<meta name="theme-color" content="#0a0a0a">`, an apple-touch-icon link (`<link rel="apple-touch-icon" ... href="/apple-icon.png">`, 180x180), `<meta name="apple-mobile-web-app-title" content="tvlog">`, and both `apple-mobile-web-app-capable`/`mobile-web-app-capable` = `yes`. The favicon is still `/icon.svg` (unchanged).
- **Icons resolve** — each icon URL returns `200` with an `image/*` content-type: `/icons/icon-192.png`, `/icons/icon-512.png`, `/icons/icon-maskable-512.png`, and `/apple-icon.png`. They share the app mark (a dark rounded TV screen with three dots, one lime).
- **Install prompt** — a compact dark banner fixed near the bottom of the screen (`role="region"`, aria-label **"Install tvlog"**) reading **"Install tvlog"** / "Add it to your home screen for one-tap logging.", shown to both logged-in and logged-out visitors on every page **except `/install`** (where it is suppressed). It sits above the search FAB when the FAB is on screen (signed in, phone width, not `/search`) and otherwise sits at the bottom edge, and it carries an **Install** button on Chromium (which fires the platform install dialog; if the prompt can't be shown it navigates to `/install` instead) or, on iOS Safari, a **"Tap Share, then 'Add to Home Screen'"** instruction line ending in **"How →"** in its place — on iOS the banner's text area is a **link to `/install`** (the walkthrough above), so tapping anywhere except the × opens the walkthrough; a **×** ("Not now") dismisses it and records that in `localStorage` under `tvlog.installPromptDismissed`, so it stays gone on later loads. **It will normally NOT appear to the collector, and that is the correct outcome, not a failure**: headless Chromium does not fire `beforeinstallprompt`, and the collector's UA is not iOS, so neither branch activates and the component renders nothing at all. It is also suppressed whenever the app is already running standalone. Judge it by its absence being silent — no console noise, no network requests, nothing overlapping the FAB or the log sheet (it sits below the sheet's z-layer).
- **Service worker + offline floor** — in a production build, `/sw.js` registers (scope `/`) and becomes the page's controller. It is intentionally minimal: it precaches only a static offline page (`/offline.html`, which reads "tvlog is offline") and serves it **only** when a top-level navigation fails on the network. It caches no app content and **never intercepts API, auth, or asset requests** — so signing in, searching, opening a show, and logging a watch must all behave exactly as without the SW (no stale data, no failed requests, no extra console noise). Registration is production-only, so it will not appear under `next dev`.
